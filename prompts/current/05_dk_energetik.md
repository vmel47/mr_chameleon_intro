SCENE CONTEXT
He walks across the central square of an abandoned city, past the long façade of a Palace of Culture. He
does not slow down, does not stop and does not react to it. He has crossed this square thousands of times.

GAZE
The gaze is straight ahead along the route for most of the take. It moves exactly ONCE: to the right, at
seven seconds, and back at thirteen. Nothing else — no second adjustment, no correction, no small extra
move in either direction.
That one movement is a human head movement, not a camera pan. It turns about thirty degrees to the RIGHT
over a second and a half onto the façade, rests there while the body keeps walking, and comes back over two
seconds. The eyes arrive a fraction before the head. It accelerates quickest through the first third of the
movement and eases out longer at the end, never at a constant rate, and settles when it arrives with the
faintest give. While it is held it is not locked or frozen — it carries a fraction of a degree of living
wander, and it eases a little further round as the body walks on, because holding your eyes on something
you are walking past requires the neck to keep giving. The head tips very slightly downward through the
middle of each turn and comes back up, because real heads travel on an arc and not on a flat plane. Never a
constant-speed pan, never a mechanical sweep, never a snap, never a linear rotation.
The walk does not react to it at all: the same pace, the same rhythm, straight on underneath the turn.

COLLIDER — the camera is a body, not a flying camera
The camera has a physical body attached to it. The body is never seen, but it occupies space and it has
mass, and nothing in the world passes through it.
Do not place anything in the path. Do not invent saplings, branches or obstacles in front of the camera —
the way ahead along the route stays clear and open, and the vegetation lives at the sides of frame.
But if anything does end up in the path — a sapling, a clump of tall grass, a low branch, a shrub — it
reacts. It bends, shakes and folds away where the unseen body meets it, and it recovers a beat later. It is
never passed through as if it were not there.
Grass and weeds underfoot bend and flatten where each step lands, then spring back after the foot lifts,
one beat behind the walk. Dust and pollen lift from the ground where the foot lands.
No arms, no hands, no shoulders, no legs and no feet ever enter frame. What is seen is the vegetation
reacting, never the body that made it react.
No clipping. Nothing intersects or passes through the camera, and nothing touches the lens.

PHYSICS

Cloud moves at cloud speed. The building is dead static mass and does not move at all — only the vegetation
on and around it moves. Perspective shifts on the façade are produced by the walk alone.
