# 05 · ПАРК · две генерации · от первого лица

Разбито надвое.

**`05A` — АВТОДРОМ, 15 сек.** Он идёт по мощёной аллее и **с первого кадра уже смотрит влево, на машинки.**
Проходит вдоль всего павильона, потом выравнивает взгляд вперёд — и в конце аллеи стоит колесо. Три
секунды на него, и кадр обрывается на ходу. `VO-04` здесь.

**`05B` — КОЛЕСО, 15 сек.** Следующим кадром оно уже вблизи.

**Почему хвост короткий.** Раньше у меня после раскрытия было восемь секунд подхода к колесу. Это ошибка:
кадр показал новое и дальше просто едет к нему. Открытие живёт ровно столько, сколько его не разжёвывают —
две-три секунды, и режем на крупный. Момент удивления надо забирать, пока он ещё удивление, а не пейзаж.

**Почему машинки первыми.** Автодром маленький и на уровне глаз: видно отдельные машинки, мох, медальоны
на ограде. Колесо двадцать шесть метров и читается на фоне неба. От малого к большому — подъём; наоборот —
сдувание. Плюс колесо это икона катастрофы: покажешь первым, и всё после него сноска.

**Почему взгляд не уходит, а возвращается.** Он смотрит на машинки уже в первом кадре — мы не видим, как
он начал смотреть, мы застаём его смотрящим. И колесо появляется не потому, что он его заметил, а потому
что **перестал смотреть на машинки**. Раскрытие как побочный эффект. Ничего не подчёркнуто.

# 05A · АВТОДРОМ

**Модель:** Seedance 2.5 · 16:9 · **15s**
**Референс:** фотография автодрома с красной стрелкой — в промпте она называется **IMAGE 1**

`VO-04` на **+4.0s**: *"I like to walk here. It is so quiet. Nobody bothers me."*

```

and no cast shadow of the viewer enters frame at any moment. We only ever see what he sees.

SCENE CONTEXT
He walks along a paved avenue through the abandoned amusement park in Pripyat. The derelict bumper-car ride
runs along the LEFT side of the avenue and he walks its whole length. He is already looking at it when the
shot begins. He does not slow down, does not stop and does not react — he walks this way often.

IMAGE 1 — the real place, and the route
IMAGE 1 is a photograph of the actual ride and the actual avenue. Take the place from it exactly:
The bumper-car ride, its roof a row of rusted steel lattice trusses — shallow arched girders of thin angle
iron with fine diagonal bracing, one after another down its length, corroded deep orange-brown. THE ROOF
COVERING IS COMPLETELY GONE: only open sky and green tree crowns show between the trusses. Conical enamel
lamp shades still hanging from the girders, tilted and dead. Slender square columns, chalky and streaked.
A dark metal deck floor taken back by moss in broad green sheets, grass out of every seam, leaf litter.
Four or five small round bumper cars standing on it where they were left, faded to chalky pale yellow, dull
green and washed-out pink, split seats, one steering wheel still upright.
Along its near edge a perimeter fence of thin steel tube railing with round embossed metal medallions set
into it at intervals, all of it rusted, some sections leaning.
The avenue outside the railing: large square concrete paving slabs, grass through every joint, dappled tree
shadow. Dense birch and poplar in full leaf pressing in from every side and growing through the structure
itself.
Far down the avenue, the yellow lattice Ferris wheel standing against the sky.

THE RED ARROW DRAWN ON IMAGE 1 IS THE WALKING ROUTE, NOT AN OBJECT. He walks along the paving in the
direction it points, with the ride on his LEFT throughout. The arrow is an annotation and must never appear
in the shot — no red line, no marking, no paint on the ground, nothing drawn.

DO NOT take the weather from IMAGE 1. Its sky is bright and hard; ours is half broken cloud with a soft sun
behind thin cloud, described below. Do not take its camera position or its framing either — the camera is a
walking person at eye level, about 1.7 metres from the ground, moving along the avenue.

ENVIRONMENT
Season: late spring turning to early summer. Birch, poplar and grass in full new green, everything
overgrown, everything alive.
Sky: half broken cloud, slow-moving, with gaps of pale blue.
Sun: high and soft behind thin cloud, warm directional light sitting high and three-quarters behind, soft
warm rim on edges, camera side a stop and a half under.
Shadows: soft-edged and long, all falling the same way, dappled through the trees onto the paving.
Air: light haze thickening with distance, pollen drifting in the bright gaps.
Time: 1:00 PM, early afternoon daylight.

COLOUR AND GRADE
Neutral daylight white balance, approximately 5600K. No warming filter, no cooling filter, no colour cast
of any kind.
Natural saturation — foliage reads true green, concrete reads neutral grey, sky reads pale blue. No
teal-and-orange, no bleach bypass, no film emulation LUT, no stylised grade.
Exposure held constant across the whole shot: mid-tones open and clearly readable, highlights in the sky
retained and never clipped, shadows dark but never crushed to black — detail visible everywhere in frame.
Medium contrast. Bright, clear, natural summer daylight, recorded the way an ordinary camera records it.
NO VIGNETTE. No edge darkening, no corner falloff, no lens shading — even exposure corner to corner across
the entire frame.

FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING
Already walking, and ALREADY LOOKING AT THE BUMPER CARS. The shot does not begin looking forward — it
begins with the gaze already turned about thirty-five degrees to the left, and what it is turned onto is
specific: the small round bumper cars standing on the mossy metal deck. We never see the movement that put
it there.
IN THE FIRST FRAME, one bumper car sits at the CENTRE of frame, ten to twelve metres away — a low round
body with a rolled rim, faded chalky paint, split seat, moss creeping up its side. That car is the subject
of the frame, not the building around it.
Around it: the medallioned railing running across the lower third of frame at an angle, the mossy deck
behind it, the rusted trusses across the top with sky and green leaves between them, the paving of the
avenue at the lower right where the feet are.
The direction of travel is off to the RIGHT of frame, only partly visible at the edge.
No empty establishing beat and no held opening frame: the walk is already in progress in the first frame.

FORMAT MODE
ONE single continuous unbroken take. No cuts of any kind — no hard cut, no jump cut, no transition, no
dissolve, no change of camera position, no separate shots. The whole fifteen seconds is one uninterrupted
walk from one camera.

OPTICS
47° horizontal field of view — normal, the way an eye reads a space. Deep focus: the railing at arm's length
and the far end of the avenue are both sharp. No rack focus, no shallow depth of field, no lens flare, no
anamorphic streaks, no fisheye, no barrel distortion at the edges.

CAMERA
The camera is a walking body moving at a slow, calm, unhurried gait, not a rig: gentle vertical rise and
fall on each stride, small lateral sway, organic imperfect correction after every step. It never stabilises
into a glide. No push, no zoom, no dolly, no arc, no orbit. It walks one straight line down the avenue and
never turns off it, never approaches the ride, never steps over the railing.
The body walks FORWARD the whole time even while the head is turned left. The world slides past accordingly:
the trusses, the cars and the railing all travel steadily across frame from right to left because he is
moving, not because the camera is panning.

GAZE
WHAT HE IS LOOKING AT, exactly: the bumper cars. Not the trees, not the sky, not the roof structure, not
an empty stretch of deck. The cars themselves — the small round bodies standing on the metal floor. For the
whole first ten seconds there is ALWAYS a bumper car at or near the centre of frame. As he walks, the
nearest car drifts out at the left edge and the next one along comes into the middle, and the head eases
slowly further round to keep a car centred. If at any moment the centre of frame holds only empty deck or
only trees, the shot is wrong.
He is looking at them the way you look at something familiar while walking past it — steadily, without
curiosity, without stopping.

The gaze moves exactly ONCE in the whole take, and it is a RETURN, not a departure: it is already on the
cars when the shot starts, and at ten seconds it comes back to the direction of travel and stays there.
Nothing else — no second adjustment, no correction, no small extra move.
That movement is a human head movement, not a camera pan. It turns about thirty-five degrees to the RIGHT
over two seconds, back to straight ahead. The eyes arrive a fraction before the head. It accelerates
quickest through the first third and eases out longer at the end, never at a constant rate, and settles
when it arrives with the faintest give. The head tips very slightly downward through the middle of the turn
and comes back up, because real heads travel on an arc and not on a flat plane. Never a constant-speed pan,
never a mechanical sweep, never a snap, never a linear rotation.
Before that, while the gaze rests on the ride, it is not locked or frozen: it carries a fraction of a degree
of living wander, and it eases slowly further round as the body walks past, because holding your eyes on
something you are walking alongside requires the neck to keep giving.

ACTION TIMING
0.0–10.0s — walking down the avenue at an unhurried, calm, leisurely cinematic pace, gaze already on the
BUMPER CARS to the LEFT and staying on them. One car is centred in frame at all times: as he draws level
with it, it drifts out at the left edge and the next car along arrives in the middle, and the head eases
slowly further round to hold it. Four or five cars pass this way, one after another. Around them the mossy
deck slides by, truss after truss crosses overhead with sky and green between them, the medallioned railing
travels across the bottom of frame, and branches reach in over it and pass close.
10.0–12.0s — the gaze comes back to the direction of travel and settles.
12.0–15.0s — and there, straight ahead at the far end of the avenue, the FERRIS WHEEL: a yellow lattice
wheel standing against the sky above the treeline, gondolas hanging dead still in a ring, small at this
distance. He does not react to it in any way — no pause, no second look, no change of pace. Three seconds,
and the shot ends while the walk is still going. It does not slow, does not settle and does not resolve.

COLLIDER — the camera is a body, not a flying camera
The camera has a physical body attached to it. The body is never seen, but it occupies space and it has
mass, and nothing in the world passes through it.
Do not place anything in the path. Do not invent branches or obstacles in front of the camera — the avenue
ahead stays clear, and the vegetation lives at the sides of frame.
But the branches over the railing hang close to the route, and if one does end up in the path it bends,
shakes and folds away where the unseen body meets it, and recovers a beat later. It is never passed through
as if it were not there.
Grass and weeds in the paving joints bend and flatten where each step lands, then spring back after the
foot lifts, one beat behind the walk. Dust and pollen lift where the foot lands.
No arms, no hands, no shoulders, no legs and no feet ever enter frame. What is seen is the vegetation
reacting, never the body that made it react.
No clipping. Nothing intersects or passes through the camera, and nothing touches the lens.

PHYSICS
Weight transfer on every slow step: heel contact, mass settling, toe push-off. Consistent footfall bounce
on hard uneven concrete slabs, with a slightly different tilt where a slab has heaved. Wind moves the grass
and the branches on real wind, the leaves on a separate faster frequency. Cloud moves at cloud speed.
NOTHING ON THE RIDE MOVES. The bumper cars do not roll, do not rock and do not shift by a millimetre. The
trusses do not sway. The railing does not move. The lamp shades hang dead still on their brackets. The
machinery has been seized for forty years and the wind moves only the vegetation. Every apparent change in
the ride is produced by the walk alone, by perspective.

LIGHTING
Late afternoon daylight filtered by the trees. Soft sun high and three-quarters behind: warm rim along the
top of every truss, the top edge of the railing and the tops of the grass, while the interior of the ride
falls into green shade so the trusses read as rusted lines against bright leaves and sky. Dappled shadow
travelling across the paving as he walks. The light level is constant across the whole take and never drops.

AUDIO
Diegetic environmental sound only — footsteps on hard concrete paving, wind through heavy summer foliage
close on both sides, one loose piece of metal ticking somewhere in the structure, sparse distant bird calls,
drifting insects. No creaking ride, no turning machinery, no music, no fairground sound of any kind. No
narration. No voices. Nobody speaks.

STYLE
Style: 8K IMAX. Photorealistic — no 3D render, no game engine, no game-cutscene aesthetic. Documentary realism of an abandoned place, not a dressed set.
Cinematography: floating immersive camera that lives inside the scene; natural motivated light only; painterly composed frames, strong silhouettes against the light.
Lighting: Late spring afternoon at 1:00 PM under half broken cloud. Soft sun high and three-quarters behind the subject — warm rim on every edge, camera side a stop and a half under, never blinding, no hard flare. Key light from sky only. No fill from the camera side.
Color: 60:30:10 — 60% new-growth green, 30% weathered concrete grey and pale sky, 10% rust-orange and faded lemon yellow.
Camera: Physical cine lens. 180° shutter motion blur.
Surfaces: Material-level realism — corroded angle iron, chalky flaking paint, moss in sheets, split seats, leaf litter, heaved concrete paving, decades of dirt. Nothing looks recently placed.
Physics: Gravity and inertia respected — mass has real weight, correct contact shadows. No floating props. Nothing passes through anything — no clipping, no intersection; whatever is touched bends, shakes and recovers a beat later.
Vegetation: Birch and poplar reclaiming the park — growing through the structure itself, branches over the railing, grass out of the metal deck and every paving joint, all of it moving on real wind.
Composition: Rule of thirds + golden ratio.
Continuity: Environment identical from the first frame to the last. No drift.
Technical: 24fps smooth motion. 8K detail. No jitter. No flicker. No vignette, no edge darkening, even exposure corner to corner.
Audio: Environmental SFX only. No music. No subtitles. No narration. No voices.

QUALITY
8K detail. Corroded angle iron, moss, chalky paint and rust at material level. Clean cloud gradation. No
jitter, no flicker.

POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS
Zero people visible anywhere. No hands, no arms, no feet, no body part and no cast shadow of the viewer
enters frame at any time.
No red arrow, no drawn line, no marking, no annotation, no paint and no graphic of any kind anywhere in the
picture.
The slow walk never stops, never slows and never speeds up — the body keeps moving forward from the first
frame to the last, including while the head is turned.
ONE continuous take with ZERO cuts.
The shot BEGINS with the gaze already on the ride. There is no opening beat looking forward and no movement
that turns the head onto it — we join him already looking.
The gaze is on the BUMPER CARS for the whole first ten seconds — there is always a car at or near the
centre of frame, never only empty deck, never only trees, never only the roof structure.
The gaze moves exactly ONCE: back to the direction of travel at 10.0s, over two seconds, and it stays
there. Nothing else. No second look at the ride, no glance at the wheel, no correction.
He stays on the avenue. He never turns off it, never approaches the ride, never steps over the railing and

The ride is on the LEFT for the whole take.
NOTHING ON THE RIDE MOVES. The bumper cars never roll or rock, the trusses never sway, the lamp shades hang

The roof covering is entirely gone — only trusses, sky and leaves overhead. Never a solid roof, never a
closed shed.
The Ferris wheel appears only after 12.0s, straight ahead at the far end of the avenue, small and distant.
It is never approached, never centred as an event and never reacted to. It gets three seconds and no more,

Half broken cloud, never a clear cloudless sky.
Late spring, full new green. Not autumn, not winter, no bare branches, no brown grass.
Nothing passes through the camera or the body carrying it — no clipping, no intersection, nothing touching
the lens.
No vignette, no edge darkening, even exposure corner to corner.
```

# ЧТО ПРОВЕРИТЬ В `05A`

| Симптом | Что делать |
|---|---|
| Кадр начинается взглядом вперёд, потом поворот влево | Главное. Усилить `The shot BEGINS with the gaze already on the ride`, `we join him already looking` |
| Взгляд возвращается и снова уходит на колесо | Усилить `no glance at the wheel`, `the gaze moves exactly ONCE` |
| Колесо появляется слишком рано или крупно | Усилить `appears only after 12.0s, small and distant` |
| У павильона сплошная крыша | Усилить `THE ROOF COVERING IS COMPLETELY GONE` |
| Машинки катаются, фермы качаются | Усилить `NOTHING ON THE RIDE MOVES`, `seized for forty years` |
| Камера панорамирует вместо ходьбы | Усилить `they travel across frame because he is moving, not because the camera is panning` |
| В кадре красная стрелка | Запрет стоит дважды; если пролезло — добавить третий раз в конец |
