SCENE CONTEXT
He walks along a paved avenue through the abandoned amusement park in Pripyat. The derelict bumper-car ride
runs along the LEFT side of the avenue and he walks its whole length. He is already looking at it when the

What is in it, so it is not lost:

Four or five small round bumper cars standing on it where they were left, faded to chalky pale yellow, dull

Along its near edge a perimeter fence of thin steel tube railing with round embossed metal medallions set
into it at intervals, all of it rusted, some sections leaning.
The avenue outside the railing: large square concrete paving slabs, grass through every joint, strong

Far down the avenue, the yellow lattice Ferris wheel standing against the sky.
Bright summer afternoon, blue sky with scattered white cumulus, sun fairly high, warm light through heavy

Both arrows are annotations, not objects. Neither may ever appear in the picture — no red line, no blue

COLOUR AND GRADE
Neutral daylight white balance, approximately 5600K. No warming filter, no cooling filter, no colour cast

Natural saturation — foliage reads true green, concrete reads neutral grey, sky reads pale blue. No

NO VIGNETTE. No edge darkening, no corner falloff, no lens shading — even exposure corner to corner across

No empty establishing beat and no held opening frame: the walk is already in progress in the first frame.

ONE single continuous unbroken take. No cuts of any kind — no hard cut, no jump cut, no transition, no

The whole take is ONE evenly flowing movement. The forward travel runs at one constant velocity from the
first frame to the last: it never surges, never hitches, never stalls, never pauses and never repeats a

NOTHING JUMPS. No snap, no jolt, no stutter, no hop, no teleport, no sudden reposition, no frame that does

There are no waypoints, no marks to hit and no staged beats. The motion is one smooth continuous curve from
beginning to end, not a series of steps between positions.

OPTICS
47° horizontal field of view — normal, the way an eye reads a space. Deep focus: the railing at arm's length

He walks along the line marked by the red arrow on @Image 1: on the paving OUTSIDE the railing, roughly six

and never approaches the ride. The direction of travel is constant for the whole take.
Eye level of a walking adult, about 1.7 metres from the ground.

CAMERA ORIENTATION — where it points, and when that changes
Horizontal, left and right: THE CAMERA STARTS ALONG THE BLUE ARROW ON @Image 1 — turned well round to the

does not move again. That is the ONLY horizontal movement in the whole shot.
Vertical, up and down: tipped slightly DOWN, about two and a half degrees, for as long as the view is on
the ride, because the deck and the cars are below eye level. As it turns back to the direction of travel
the tilt comes back to level and stays level to the end.
Roll: none. The horizon stays level for the whole take, including through the turn. No tilt of the frame at
any point.

HOW THAT TURN MUST FEEL
It is a neck under a skull, not a motor on an axis. The eyes arrive a fraction before the head. It
accelerates quickest through the first third of the movement and eases out longer at the end, never at a

never a mechanical sweep, never a snap, never a linear rotation.
The walk does not react to it: the same pace, the same rhythm, straight on underneath the turn.

CAMERA
The camera is a walking body moving at a slow, calm, unhurried gait, not a rig: gentle vertical rise and
fall on each stride, small lateral sway, organic imperfect correction after every step. It never stabilises
into a glide. No push, no zoom, no dolly, no arc, no orbit. It follows the arrow in ONE straight line along
the avenue, parallel to the ride, and never turns off it, never approaches the ride, never crosses the
railing, never steps onto the deck.
The pace is constant from the first frame to the last and does not change when the view turns.

ACTION TIMING
0.0–10.0s — walking down the avenue at an unhurried, calm, leisurely cinematic pace, the camera held at the
blue-arrow angle, on the bumper-car ride, and staying there. He is simply walking past the ride and
looking at it, square into it rather than past it. The Ferris wheel is nowhere in the picture. The whole ride passes across the frame from right to left as he goes: the medallioned railing travelling along
the bottom of frame, the mossy deck behind it with the bumper cars standing on it, truss after truss
crossing overhead with sky and green leaves between them, branches reaching in over the railing and passing
close. Everything moves because he is walking, not because the camera is panning.
10.0–12.0s — the view turns back to the direction of travel and settles.
12.0–15.0s — and there, straight ahead at the far end of the avenue, the FERRIS WHEEL: a yellow lattice

distance. He does not react to it in any way — no pause, no second look, no change of pace. Three seconds,
and the shot ends while the walk is still going. It does not slow, does not settle and does not resolve.

COLLIDER — the camera is a body, not a flying camera
The camera has a physical body attached to it. The body is never seen, but it occupies space and it has
mass, and nothing in the world passes through it.
Do not place anything in the path. Do not invent branches or obstacles in front of the camera — the avenue
ahead stays clear, and the vegetation lives at the sides of frame.
But the branches over the railing hang close to the route, and if one does end up in the path — a branch, a
clump of tall grass, a sapling — it reacts. It bends, shakes and folds away where the unseen body meets it,
and it recovers a beat later. It is never passed through as if it were not there.
Grass and weeds underfoot bend and flatten where each step lands, then spring back after the foot lifts,
one beat behind the walk. Dust and pollen lift from the ground where the foot lands.
No arms, no hands, no shoulders, no legs and no feet ever enter frame. What is seen is the vegetation
reacting, never the body that made it react.
No clipping. Nothing intersects or passes through the camera, and nothing touches the lens.

PHYSICS
Weight transfer on every slow step: heel contact, mass settling, toe push-off. Consistent footfall bounce
on hard uneven concrete paving, with a slightly different tilt where a slab has heaved. Wind moves the grass
in travelling waves and the poplar leaves on a separate faster frequency. Cloud moves at cloud speed.

trusses do not sway. The railing does not move. The lamp shades hang dead still on their brackets. The
machinery has been seized for forty years and the wind moves only the vegetation. Every apparent change in
the ride is produced by the walk alone, by perspective.

Late afternoon daylight filtered by the trees. Soft sun high and three-quarters behind: warm rim along the
top of every truss, the top edge of the railing and the tops of the grass, while the interior of the ride
