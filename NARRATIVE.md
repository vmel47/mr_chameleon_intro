# MR. CHAMELEON — NARRATIVE BIBLE

The story, the character and the rules of the phenomenon. Everything in `SCRIPT_EN.md`, `SHOTLIST_PROMPTS.md` and every asset descriptor has to obey this file. If a shot contradicts this document, the shot is wrong.

---

## 1. LOGLINE

A man walks out of the forest into Pripyat and gives a calm, affectionate tour of his home town — a city that has been dead for forty years. Nothing threatens him. Nothing happens to him. He is simply, comfortably, catastrophically at home. This is the origin story of what he becomes.

## 2. WHAT THIS FILM IS

**A first-person guided tour of a grave, narrated by someone who does not know it is one.**

The horror is not in events. There is no threat, no chase, no monster, no jump scare until the final seconds. The horror is the **gap between what the audience sees and what the narrator says about it.**

The audience sees a radioactive ruin. He says *"my favourite city, you are more beautiful than I remember you."*
The audience sees a rotted flat with a tree growing through the window. He says *"this is our living room."*
The audience sees an amusement park that has not moved since 1986. He says *"I like to walk here. It is so quiet. Nobody bothers me."*

Every one of those lines is sincere. He is not being ironic. He is not performing. He means it, and that is what makes it unbearable.

## 3. THE CHARACTER

**Who he is.** As a child he was left behind in the zone during the evacuation. He survived alone, moved on his own, and eventually got himself out. Nobody came for him. Nobody has ever fully explained what happened to him in the years since.

**What Pripyat is to him.** Home. Not a trauma site, not a place he is confronting — literally the place he is from and still thinks of as his. He comes back the way anyone visits the town they grew up in. He walks a route he knows. He does not look around like a tourist and he does not brace like a survivor.

**What is wrong with him.** He is adequate on the surface and off underneath. He can hold a thought, follow a route, describe a room accurately. What is broken is not his reasoning — it is his relationship to what is normal. He is missing the reflex that would make a person recoil.

**How this must be played:** never as madness. No twitching, no muttering, no wild eyes, no unstable behaviour. He is calm, quiet, still, and completely at ease. **The audience must be the only one in the room who thinks something is wrong.** The moment the performance signals "this man is disturbed", the film collapses into genre and stops being frightening.

**His single tell:** total absence of the small adjustments people make when they are being watched or when they are somewhere unpleasant. He does not brace, does not scan, does not check behind himself, does not react to decay. He walks into a mould-blackened kitchen exactly the way you walk into yours.

## 4. THE PHENOMENON — THE SIGNS

**What they are.** He perceives a dense structured pattern on the surface of living things — on other people's skin, and eventually on his own. It is not a hallucination he suffers; it is information he reads.

**Where the look comes from.** The visual reference is the geometric imagery reported under psychedelics — what researchers call **form constants**: lattices, gratings, honeycombs, tunnels, spirals and webbing. Highly regular, tightly tiled, high frequency, organised. People who report it commonly describe it as "like the code in The Matrix" — that comparison is the *feeling* to hit, not the design to copy.

**Hard rules for how the signs appear on screen:**

| The signs ARE | The signs are NOT |
|---|---|
| Structure under the skin, deforming with the body | An overlay, projection or screen effect |
| Geometric, tiled, high-frequency, regular | Letters, numerals, glyphs, any alphabet, any script |
| Low contrast, tonal, almost a watermark | Glowing, emissive, coloured, green |
| Something only he can see | Something that lights the room or casts shadow |
| Present on skin only | Present on clothing, walls, objects, air |

**Why the rules matter.** The instant those marks glow green and scroll like film code, the audience files the whole film under "superhero VFX" and stops being disturbed. Kept as a physical thing under skin, it stays deniable — which is where all the dread in this project lives.

**What he does with them.** He can read the pattern on a person, rearrange the same pattern on himself to match, and become them. In this film he only *discovers* he can. He does it once, in a mirror, and the film ends there.

## 5. THE ARC — AND WHAT IS DELIBERATELY MISSING

This film is **part one: the origin.** It is not the whole story, and it deliberately refuses to explain itself.

**In this film:**

- He returns.
- He shows us his city and his home, and we understand that something is deeply wrong with him.
- We learn, in one memory, that he began to see things in a basement.
- We see him take another person's shape for the first time.

**Not in this film, and must not be hinted at with exposition:**

- Who put him in that basement, and why.
- What was done to him there.
- How he got out.
- Anything about what he becomes later.

The basement memory arrives with no setup and no explanation — a person seated in a room, an employee working, and then something impossible. The audience is left to assemble it. **If a shot starts explaining, cut the shot.**

## 6. THE INTENDED EFFECT

Cinematic to a level that makes people uncomfortable with something that is, on paper, just a man walking around. The shock has to be **earned by contrast, not by content**: four minutes of the most beautiful, patient, documentary photography of a dead city, over a voice that finds it lovely — and then one impossible thing at the very end.

The film should leave the audience with two facts and no explanation:

1. This man is not what he appears to be.
2. He is completely comfortable with that.

## 7. TONE LAWS

1. **Documentary before cinema.** Everything must look observed rather than staged. No dressing, no arranged objects, no lit-for-camera. If a frame looks designed, it is wrong.
2. **Deniable before spectacular.** Any moment that could be a trick of light should be left as one. Only the mirror is unambiguous.
3. **No genre signalling.** No horror score, no stingers, no ominous camera moves, no slow menacing turns. Threat arrives without announcement, or not at all.
4. **Sound is the world, not the mood.** Environmental only, always continuous. The only human voice in the entire film is the scream at the end.
5. **He never addresses the audience.** He is not telling us anything. The narration is a man talking to himself in the place he grew up. We happen to hear it.
6. **Real places only.** Pripyat is real, its landmarks are real, and audiences recognise them. That recognition is doing the work — the viewer already knows the place is dead before he says a word about it. Invented locations lose that for free.

## 8. THE VOICE

Flat, unhurried, quiet. Not sinister, not sad, not nostalgic. He is not performing calm — he simply is calm. He never raises his voice, never speeds up, never colours a line.

Lines are short and domestic: *this is my room, this is our living room, and this is the kitchen.* The banality is the weapon. The one line that carries any warmth is *"home, sweet home"*, and it should be delivered without a smile behind it.

## 9. WHAT THE FILM MUST NEVER DO

- Never show him reacting to decay, danger, or radiation.
- Never let another living person appear anywhere in the city.
- Never show his face until the ending decides to show it.
- Never make the signs legible, glowing, or explained.
- Never use music.
- Never let the narration comment on his own condition. He does not know.
- Never resolve anything.
